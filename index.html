Native guider

Long focal length, without the fight.

At 2800 mm a single arcsecond of error spreads across several pixels, and a guider that thinks in pixels gets every threshold wrong. Alan Astro's guider thinks in arcseconds of sky, so the same settings mean the same thing on a RedCat and on an EdgeHD 11.

  • Thresholds in arcseconds

    The minimum move, settle tolerance and star search window are set in arcseconds of sky, with sensible floors, then converted to your pixel scale.

  • Knows your guide optics

    Guide scope, off-axis guider, or the ASI2600MC Duo's built-in guide sensor, with binning taken into account. It won't start guiding without a pixel scale.

  • Five stars, median shift

    It guides on several stars at once and uses the median of their shifts, so one star in bad seeing can't drag the mount.

  • Picks stars by contrast

    It ranks candidate stars by how far they stand out from the background, not by raw brightness. It can also avoid the distorted stars at the edges of an off-axis guider's field. On one field, that choice alone was the difference between 3.38″ and 1.29″ RMS.

  • A guiding assistant

    It watches the mount drift unguided, separates seeing from drift, and suggests a minimum move in arcseconds.

One arcsecond of tracking error on a 3.76 µm sensor. At 2,800 mm it covers twelve times as many pixels as at 240 mm, so a threshold set in pixels would be off by the same factor. That's why the guider works in arcseconds.

And the rest of it

Everything else, done properly.

Autofocus that measures backlash before trusting it

A coarse search, then a fine pass, with a hyperbola fitted through the samples and R² reported. It measures the focuser's backlash, won't sweep one whose slack has never been measured, and learns the focus offset for each filter. When a run fails, it says so.

0.12′

Plate solving that catches mistakes

ASTAP runs locally. It centred to 0.12 arcminutes in two attempts, and it reports the focal length it measured, so it notices when you forgot to fit the reducer.

  • Solves offline, no internet
  • Centres to a tolerance you set
  • Labels what's in the frame
  • Measured vs profile focal length

Polar alignment

A three-point routine that shows the correction on the bolts you actually turn: altitude and azimuth, with a direction and a size.

Live stacking

Frames are aligned on their stars, with 5σ outlier rejection and an HFR gate. When it rejects a frame, it tells you why. It can also stack a running plan as it goes.

Star analysis

HFR, star count and eccentricity for every frame, plus a tilt map that shows which way stars elongate across the field.

A sky atlas that knows your horizon

12,520 objects from OpenNGC plus a curated Messier, Caldwell and Abell set. It filters by what clears your own horizon tonight, and shows each target's altitude curve, transit time and distance from the Moon. There's a mosaic planner too.

Safe when you're asleep

Six weather limits (wind, gusts, rain, cloud, humidity and dew margin) pause the night and resume it when conditions clear, instead of ending it. A safety monitor that stops responding counts as unsafe. Your own horizon file applies to every slew.
